Part I · The current scaffold

What Oot is today

A working Rust seed that adjudicates a Change between two snapshots.

  1. 01

    EngineStructural diff engine

  2. Rust + tree-sitter-rust. diff_snapshots(base, head) walks both snapshots and emits Meaning disputes for functions changed, added, or removed.
  3. Takes byte blobs, not file paths. The materialized tree is optional, which is what lets it run in memory later.

  4. 02

    ModelDispute and Docket

  5. Kind is Meaning | Visibility. Verdict is Adjudicated | Blocked | Embargoed | Cloaked.
  6. The renderer prints the docket artifact that ships in the README, so the CLI output and the docs agree.

  7. 03

    PolicyVisibility, in alpha

  8. VisibilityPolicy holds private_paths and embargo_until, and emits Visibility disputes for touched private paths.
  9. Still missing: per-author allowlists and branch-level privacy. Those are the next build steps, not rewrites.

  10. 04

    CLIoot adjudicate

  11. Flags: --base / --head snapshot dirs, --source, --authors, --policy, --visibility, and --docket to replay a saved record.
  12. Both paths are verified: live adjudication and docket replay render the same shape.

  13. 05

    EvidenceAn honest problem base

    27.67%of AI-agent pull requests hit merge conflicts AgenticFlict, arXiv:2604.03551
    26×higher bug risk for semantic merge conflicts Brindescu et al., Empirical SE 2020

  14. The original README's four statistics were fabricated. They were removed and replaced with the figures above plus Anthropic's 2026 multi-agent turf-war study Anthropic, multiagent systems.

  15. 06

    VerifiedWhat it does on two fixtures

  16. login changed on both sides in src/lib.rs produces one meaning dispute.
  17. secrets/.env touched produces one visibility dispute and escalates the verdict to CLOAKED, with the embargo note still rendered.

Part II · After the major rethink

What Oot will be

Not a git hook. The adjudication runtime that sits above git, Jujutsu, and agents.

  1. i

    PrimitiveChange, not commit

  2. A Change is a content-addressed delta between two snapshots, from anywhere. Oot never names a branch or writes a commit message.
  3. By operating on Changes, commits and branches stop being Oot's concern. That is the rethink of them.

  4. 01

    Axis oneVisibility disputes

  5. First-class policy on paths and branches: private-to, embargo-until, public. This is the .env, monorepo-privacy, and private-branch problem, handled as policy rather than cryptography.
  6. Produces CLOAKED and EMBARGOED verdicts. This is the spine, not a side feature.

  7. 02

    Axis twoMeaning disputes

  8. Checks a Change against its declared intent. Flags merges that agree on tokens but disagree on what they mean.
  9. This is the original Oot thesis, now one axis among three rather than the whole product.

  10. 03

    PlacementVCS-agnostic, in-memory capable

  11. Reads snapshots from git or Jujutsu; never owns the repo. The engine takes byte blobs and parses trees, so it runs with no materialized working tree.
  12. An agent in a worktree cannot hold main hostage, because Oot works on snapshots, not checkouts.

  13. 04

    DelegationCrypto is not Oot's job

  14. Actual encryption delegates to git-crypt or a hosted key service. Oot owns the policy and the gate.
  15. Embargo distribution to maintainers is a hosted service, not a repo feature.

Oot's job is the court: meaning plus visibility. Not the version control, not the cryptography, not the filesystem.

    PhaseChange ingestion

  7. 04

    PhaseIn-memory execution

  8. Run the engine on snapshots with no materialized tree, for agents. Theo's cited APFS gap (30–40s macOS vs 3–12s Linux for clean installs) is the motivation, though those are his benchmarks, not independently re-run here. The fix is architectural: never touch the disk.

    PhaseHosted intent model

  10. The paid service: semantic scoring of intent and embargo distribution. The open runtime stays the gate; the model is the clerk.

The shape of the thing

Oot is not becoming a new version control system and it is not becoming a filesystem. It is the governance layer that answers the questions git and Jujutsu were never built to answer: who may see this change, what it means, and when it may go public. The original five minute meaning-only pitch is now one axis of three, and governance leads.

Everything in Part III is an extension of the seed already on disk. The engine, the docket, and the policy loader are real. What is missing is ingestion, richer visibility, the embargo scheduler, and the in-memory path.

Decision · committed

What we committed to

Your and Theo's issues outrank the original five minute pitch. The meaning-only thesis is now one axis of three.

  1. i

    CallGovernance leads, meaning follows

  2. The spine is visibility and embargo: who may see a change and when it may ship. Semantic meaning stays a first-class axis, not the whole product. README and CONTRIBUTING now lead with this.

  3. 01

    CallStateful, not only stateless

  4. Embargo and private branches need Oot to hold and release over time. That makes Oot a coordinator, not only an evaluator. The "hosted Phase 05" is now core, not an afterthought.

  5. 02

    CallBuild order re-ranked

  6. Change ingestion, then visibility policy (the spine), then meaning disputes, then docket, then in-memory, then hosted model. The original meaning-first order is retired.

The call, in one line

Oot is the governance layer for changes. The five minute "settles meaning" sketch was a starting point, not the destination. Visibility and embargo lead; meaning follows; cryptography and the VCS stay delegated.
